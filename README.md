# Willowbrook Charity — Three Horizons to Self-Sustaining

A strategic note offered as a gift to the Willowbrook Charity, exploring how it can move from grant-dependent to self-sustaining over 36 months — without losing what makes it Willowbrook.

**Live:** https://anwar1808.github.io/willowbrook-charity-brief/

## What's in it

- **Frame** — Yunus social-business posture: *mission first, surplus by design*
- **3 horizons** — 10 specific actions across 6 / 18 / 36 month windows
- **Comparators** — 6 precedents (3 UK, 3 US) that prove the model has worked
- **Corporate engagement deep-dive** — the single largest growth lever, with the Oxford-cluster scale-up case
- **Revenue projection** — Conservative / Base / Stretch scenarios across 3 years, with 5 income streams clearly defined
- **Technology layer** — 5 modest investments that compound each horizon
- **Closing** — the rarity argument

## Section ordering rationale

The 11 sections follow a deliberate arc: **question → philosophy → boundary → action → proof → biggest-lever zoom → numbers → lever-ramp → multipliers → close.**

### Six design decisions behind the order

1. **Question first, philosophy before action.** Hero poses the research question. Intro + Frame establish the *posture* (Yunus, mission-first). Constraint establishes the *boundary* (Charity Commission discipline). Only then do we get to actions. Reasoning: any specific recommendation lands harder when the reader has already accepted the underlying philosophy and constraints.

2. **Action *before* proof.** Horizons (recommendations) come before Comparators (proof). The recommendations are concrete and self-evidently sensible — they don't need pre-validation. Putting Comparators *before* would feel defensive. *After*, they read as confirmation: *"and here's why what I just suggested isn't crazy — six other charities have built variations of it."*

3. **CSR Deep-Dive between Comparators and Projection — the most contestable choice.** It interrupts the narrative rhythm. But Corporate Engagement is the *largest single lever* in the projection. Readers need to understand the lever before reading the chart. By detailing scale-ups vs big-corporates *before* the chart, the £130k Y3 corporate number reads as legible, not invented.

4. **Projection before Anchor Progression — same lever, two zoom levels.** Projection shows all 5 streams together; Anchor Progression zooms into the corporate line and shows the Y1/Y2/Y3 ramp. Preview-then-replay rhythm: the reader sees the chart, notices corporate is the largest growth contributor, then immediately gets a section explaining how that line grows year by year.

5. **Tech Layer near the end, deliberately.** Technology is a *multiplier*, not a strategy. If it appeared early, readers might think it *is* the strategy — exactly the misunderstanding to avoid. Late placement reads as "here's what compounds what we just discussed."

6. **Closing rarity argument at the end for emotional weight.** *"Willowbrook is genuinely rare"* works hardest when the reader has just absorbed the full strategic case. Earlier placement would land as a boast; at the end, it lands as a summation.

### Where the order is debatable

- **Comparators before vs after Horizons.** Currently after; proof-first is defensible for more skeptical audiences.
- **CSR Deep-Dive vs Projection sequence.** Currently CSR first; reverse (Projection → drill into the largest line) also works.
- **Anchor Progression placement.** Could merge into Projection as a sub-block.

### What should not change

- Hero → Intro → Frame → Constraint as the opening quartet — load-bearing scaffolding.
- Closing as the final beat — the rarity argument's impact depends on it.
- Tech Layer near the end — must register as multiplier, not strategy.
